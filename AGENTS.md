# Course screenshot guidance

Use wide or full screenshots in lessons and labs so readers can see where each control lives. Keep the page heading, relevant sidebar or breadcrumb, and target control in the same view whenever the layout permits. Show the navigation path nearby when it helps readers identify the page.

Before saving or publishing, remove or permanently redact names, emails, org URLs and identifiers, dates and timestamps (including Created and Last modified), credentials, and any other private details. Review the actual image pixels before adding the asset to the repository. Keep enough of the interface visible to orient the reader and provide useful alt text.

## Course lab coverage

Map every objective and configuration task in the Okta Administrator Performance Exam guide to its theory and practice. Explain what each concept does, why it matters, and how to verify it. Give learners a hands-on exercise for each configuration and a practical observation or investigation for each concept. Call out the required org features, test data, and permissions when they affect the exercise. If a task needs a different environment, document that dependency and keep the task in the coverage plan.

Keep all configuration, testing, and troubleshooting steps when a lab grows. Keep a short lab on one page when the learner benefits from seeing it all at once. Use a few numbered task tabs when they make distinct stages easier to find or keep a screenshot beside the relevant steps. When a lab has many tasks, group related tasks into named sublabs under Hands-on Lab. Aim for approximately four to five tasks per sublab and adjust the grouping when a workflow is easier to follow together. Keep the original task order and show any prerequisites or dependencies between sublabs. Place screenshots beside the steps they illustrate.

## Teaching depth and practice

Introduce named lab objects before asking the reader to use them. Say what each one is, what it does in the exercise, and whether the reader creates it here or brings it from an earlier lesson. Define essential terms and acronyms at first use in the lesson prose. Glossary popups can refresh those definitions later.

Design the theory and practice around what the reader needs to understand and do for that specific topic. Keep the Theory and Hands-on Lab tabs. Choose the order, examples, visuals, and amount of explanation based on the concept. Use a flow when sequence carries meaning, a decision table when inputs and precedence determine the result, and a UI walkthrough when finding and interpreting a control is central to the task. Introduce each term before the reader needs it. Explain the relationships in connected prose with small, neutral examples where useful. Include prerequisite platform and network terms where a lab depends on them. Add concise reminders and common aliases to the reader's course-term-help glossary as concepts are introduced. On the reader, a term's reminder opens while the learner holds the term and closes when they release it. Keep the full first explanation, worked reasoning, and needed lab instructions in the lesson itself.

Teach the concept from first principles through the administrator decisions it supports. Cover the prerequisites, lifecycle or decision behavior, configurations, meaningful variations, supported boundaries, and failure modes that affect real outcomes. Connect related topics when the reader needs that relationship to understand the result. Use the official exam guide to check every objective and configuration task. Start with its linked resources and bring in other official product documentation, platform documentation, or standards when they clarify mechanics, setup, or troubleshooting. Link the sources near the relevant material. Identify any external system, tenant feature, or test data the reader needs.

Build labs around outcomes the reader can verify. Include the setup, exact changes, realistic test inputs, expected results, and the evidence in the relevant console, target system, or logs. For topics with meaningful branches, add comparison cases, multiple inputs, or a controlled misconfiguration that the learner can diagnose, correct, and retest. Where several skills come together, add an independent practice task that gives the objective and evidence to find so the reader can work through the configuration themselves. Scale the number and difficulty of exercises to the topic. Give the reader a way to reset the test state when it affects later exercises.

Before publishing, read the theory as someone new to the concept and check whether they can explain how it works, predict the result of a configuration change, and choose where to verify it. Revisit published lessons when a later lab exposes a missing prerequisite or explanation.

## Lesson writing and production

Use GPT-6.2 Sol at medium reasoning for writing lessons, verifying Okta behavior and exam coverage, and designing hands-on labs when that model configuration is available. Use a lower-cost model for routine screenshot capture, redaction, image placement, publishing, and rendering checks when delegation is available.

Give the production pass a short brief with the lesson and repository paths, exact screens and controls to capture, redaction requirements, and what to verify after publication. Keep the final technical and privacy review with the writing pass.
